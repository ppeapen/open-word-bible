Preface to the World English Bible
What is the Holy Bible?

The Holy Bible is a collection of books and letters written by many people who were inspired by the Holy Spirit of God. These books tell us how we can be saved from the evil of this world and gain eternal life that is truly worth living. Although the Holy Bible contains rules of conduct, it is not just a rule book. It reveals God’s heart—a Father’s heart, full of love and compassion. The Holy Bible tells you what you need to know and believe to be saved from sin and evil and how to live a life that is truly worth living, no matter what your current circumstances may be.

The Holy Bible consists of two main sections: the Old Testament (including Psalms and Proverbs) and the New Testament (Matthew through Revelation). The Old Testament records God’s interaction with mankind before He sent His son to redeem us, while recording prophesy predicting that coming. The New Testament tells us of God’s Son and Anointed One, Yeshua, and the wonderful salvation that He purchased for us.

The same Holy Spirit who inspired the Holy Bible is living among us today, and He is happy to help you understand what He intended as you study His Word. Just ask Him, and He is more than happy to help you apply His message to your life.

The Old Testament was originally written mostly in Hebrew. The New Testament was originally written mostly in the common street Greek (not the formal Greek used for official legal matters). The Holy Bible is translated into many languages, and being translated into many more, so that everyone may have an opportunity to hear the Good News about Yeshua the Messiah.†“Messiah” means “Anointed One,” a King.

What is The Open Word Bible?

The Open Word Bible is a fork of the World English Bible (Classic).

There are many good translations of the Bible in English. However, not many Bible versions may be used freely in personal or commercial projects. The World English Bible (Classic edition) is a complete translation of the Holy Bible in Modern English that may be freely used without written permission from the publisher and payment of royalties.

Why create yet another English translation of the Holy Bible?

I noticed a few instances of inaccurate translation in the World English Bible. It was not possible to make those changes and retain the name "The World English Bible." Hence, a new name was given to this version.

What changes were made in The Open Word Bible?

The Open Word Bible (FWB) is derived from the World English Bible. Changes made to the classic World English Bible are listed below.

Yeshua, the Messiah
  • The proper name “Jesus” is derived from the Greek name Ιησους that was used to refer to our Savior. His actual name in Hebrew/Aramaic is Ye’shua (Yahweh is salvation). There is no reason why a proper noun should be change during its translation into another language. Therefore, I changed all occurences of “Jesus” into “Yeshua”. The use of ‘Yeshua’ goes beyond phonetic restoration; it preserves the vital theological meaning intended by God. Before His birth, the angel commanded Joseph to name Him Yeshua (meaning ‘Yahweh is salvation’) precisely because He would yoshia (‘save’) His people from their sins (Matt. 1:21). Translating this as ‘Jesus’ obscures this profound Hebrew wordplay. Because this is the name above all names, this translation seeks to honor its original, divinely appointed form.
  • Similarly, I used the word “Messiah” instead of “Christ”. Why? People have become so used to the word “Christ” that they do not think about its origin or meaning. Some might even think that it is Yeshua’s surname! I wish to emphasize the Hebrew origin of the title “Messiah” so that readers will slow down, enquire, and know that Yeshua is King.
  • Several footnotes in WEB explained the meaning of “Christ” the ‘Anointed One.’ These notes were changed to: ‘Christ’ or ‘Messiah’ literally means ‘Anointed One.’ In reality, the word refers to a King—a special king who was to be born in David’s lineage to rule forever.
  • Explanatory notes were added to Romans 9:5; Titus 2:13; and to 2 Peter 1:1 to highlight the divinity of Yeshua, the Messiah. In these verses, Paul and Peter describe the Yeshua as God.
The Divine Name: Yahweh
  • Authors of New Testament books quoted verses from the Septuagint (LXX), an ancient Greek translation of the Hebrew Scriptures. There’s a slight problem here. The Septuagint employs the Greek word Kýrios (Lord) as a surrogate for the divine name Yahweh – the Hebrew Tetragrammaton (יהוה / YHWH). They did this in accordance with second-temple Jewish traditions of qere/ketiv (where they read aloud the divine name as adonai instead of uttering the name Yahweh. When NT authors quoted verses from the LXX, the word Kýrios (Lord) got introduced into the text of the New Testament. The authors probably knew that the word “Lord” referred to Yahweh. But most of today’s readers do not know this. As a result, for instance, modern readers fail to understand the way St. John applies Hebrew passages with “Yahweh” to Yeshua. We see this in passages such as Matt 3:3; 4:4; 4:7; 4:10; Mark 1:3; Luke 3:4; 4:4; 4:8; 4:12; and John 1:23; 6:45; 12:38. I prefer the use of the divine name “Yahweh” over “Lord” as it is in the Hebrew scriptures. Why should modern readers miss out on the original Hebrew text just because the citations came via the Greek Septuagint?
    After confirming the original wording in all Hebrew scriptures cited in the New Testament, I changed the word “Lord” to “Yahweh” in these verses: Matt 3:3; 4:4; 4:7; 4:10; 5:33; 21:9; 21:42; 22:37; 22:44; Mark 1:3; 11:9; 12:11; 12:29-30; 12:36; Luke 3:4; 4:4; 4:8; 4:12; 4:18-19; 10:27; 13:35; 19:38; 20:42-43; John 1:23; John 12:13; 12:38; 12:41; Acts 2:20; 2:21; 2:25; 2:34; 3:22; 4:25-26; 7:37; 7:49; 15:17; Romans 4:3; 4:8; 9:28; 9:29; 10:13; 11:34; 15:11; 1 Cor 1:31; 2:16; 3:20; 10:26; 2 Cor 10:17; Gal 3:6; 2 Tim 2:19; Hebrews 8:8; 10:16; 10:30b; 12:6; 13:6; James 2:23; 4:6; 1 Peter 3:12; 5:5.
Translation, not mere transliteration
  • The meaning of the Greek word ekklesia is lost on readers of the English Bible because it is rendered as “Church”. The World English Bible uses the word “assembly”. I prefer the term “congregation.” In Matt 16:18, Yeshua said, “I will build my ekklesia.” This is rendered as “I will build my congregation.” The root of the word ekklesia suggests that it is a group of people who were called out (chosen and separated) to belong to a person/cause. However, to avoid the “root fallacy,” I chose the meaning prevalent in the first century. The decision to avoid the word “church” is deliberate because the word is loaded with unhelpful connotations.
  • Some words in the English Bible were transliterated from Greek instead of being translated. One such word is “baptism,” which means “immersion.” Therefore, instead of the words “baptism,” “baptize,” and “Baptizer”, I have used the words “immersion,” “immerse,” and “Immerser”, respectively.
  • Instead of using the term “apostles”, which is a transliteration of the Greek term apostolos/apostoloi, I prefer the term “envoy” or “special messenger” over the transliteration found in most Bibles. A proper translation demystifies the term.
  • The term "antichrist" is not a translation but a transliteration from Greek. The word was rendered as "The Adversary of the Messiah."
Amen: Solemn Proclamations
  • The word “Amen” is retained in most passages for the sake of its liturgical significance.
  • In the Messiah’s statements — where He used the word “Amen” to emphasize the certainty of His words — I prefer the word “truly” over the phrase “most certainly” found in WEB.
  • In the synoptic Gospels, Yeshua used “Amen” in several solemn statements. In the Fourth Gospel, we see the use of double Amen. I used “truly, truly” instead of “Amen, amen” to correctly convey the implied message. Matt 5:18, 26; 6:2, 5, 16; 8:10; 10:15, 23, 42; 11:11; 13:17; 16:28; 17:20; 18:3, 13, 18; 19:23, 28; 21:21, 31; 23:36; 24:2, 34, 47; 25:12, 40, 45; 26:13, 21, 34; Mark 3:28; 8:12; 9:1; 9:41; 10:15, 29; 11:23; 12:43; 13:30; 14:9, 18, 25, 30; Luke 4:24; 12:37, 44; 18:17, 29; 21:3, 32; 23:43; John 1:51; 3:3, 5, 11; 5:19, 24, 25; 6:26, 32, 47, 53; 8:34, 51, 58; 10:1, 7; 12:24; 13:16, 20, 21, 38; 14:12; 16:20, 23; 21:18.
Vital Corrections
  • An obvious Dispensationalist/Zionist bias is seen in the way most English Bibles translate Galatians 6:16. The World English Bible is no different. That bias was corrected by replacing it with the corresponding verse from God’s Word Translation. J. B. Phillips, RSV, and a couple of other versions too got it right.
  • Dan 9:25-26. The word “mashiach” in the Hebrew text does not have a definite article. Therefore, the phrase “the Anointed One” was changed to “an anointed prince”. The timeline suggests that the “mashiach” in v. 26 is the same as the “mashiach” in v. 25.
What are some of the features of the World English Bible that are retained in The Open Word Bible?

The Classic World English Bible translates God’s Proper Name in the Old Testament as “Yahweh.” The Open Word Bible retains the use of "Yahweh" for the tetragrammaton.

The World English Bible uses the Byzantine Majority Text (MT) as the primary basis for the New Testament. In comparison with other version of the NT, the following differences may be noted:

  • The order of Matthew 23:13 and 14 is reversed in some translations.
  • Luke 17:36 and Acts 15:34, which are not found in the majority of the Greek Manuscripts (and are relegated to footnotes in the WEB), may be included in some other translations.
  • Romans 14:24-26 in the WEB may appear as Romans 16:25-27 in other translations.
  • 1 John 5:7-8 contains an addition in some translations, including the KJV. Erasmus admitted adding this text to his published Greek New Testament, even though he could at first find no Greek manuscript support for it, because he was being pressured by men to do so, and because he didn’t see any doctrinal harm in it. Lots of things not written by John in this letter are true, but we decline to add them to what the Holy Spirit inspired through John.

With all of the above and some other places where lack of clarity in the original manuscripts has led to multiple possible readings, significant variants are listed in footnotes. The reading that in our prayerful judgment is best is in the main text. Overall, the World English Bible doesn’t differ very much from several other good contemporary English translations of the Holy Bible. The message of Salvation through Yeshua the Messiah is still the same. The point of this translation was not to be very different (except for legal status), but to update the ASV for readability while retaining or improving the accuracy of that well-respected translation and retaining the public domain status of the ASV.

Does The Open Word Bible include the Apocrypha?

The World English Bible is an ecumenical project that includes books included in Bibles in many denominations. The main 66 books of the Old and New Testaments are recognized as Scripture by all true Christians. There are also books considered to be part of, depending on which book and who you ask, Deuterocanon, Apocrypha, and Pseudepigrapha. The Deuterocanon/Apocrypha books are only included in the printed editions marked “Ecumenical”.

The following books and parts of books are recognized as Deuterocanonical Scripture by the Roman Catholic, Greek, and Russian Orthodox Churches:

    Tobit, Judith, Esther from the Greek Septuagint, The Wisdom of Solomon, Ecclesiasticus (also called The Wisdom of Yeshua Son of Sirach), Baruch, The Song of the Three Holy Children, Susanna, Bel and the Dragon, 1 Maccabees, and 2 Maccabees.

In this edition, The Letter of Jeremiah is included as chapter 6 of Baruch.

Three of those books come from parts of Daniel found in the Greek Septuagint, but not the Hebrew Old Testament: The Song of the Three Holy Children, Susanna, and Bel and the Dragon. These three are included in Daniel (Greek), in context, as they make more sense that way.

The following books are recognized as Deuterocanonical Scripture by the Greek and Russian Orthodox Churches, but not the Roman Catholic Church:

    1 Esdras, The Prayer of Manasseh, Psalm 151, and 3 Maccabees. Note that 1 Esdras and the Prayer of Manasseh are also in an appendix to the Latin Vulgate Bible.

The Slavonic Bible includes 2 Esdras, but calls it 3 Esdras. This same book is in the Appendix to the Latin Vulgate as 4 Esdras.

An appendix to the Greek Septuagint contains 4 Maccabees. It is included for its historical value.

Among Christian denominations and among individual Christians, opinions vary widely on the Deuterocanon/Apocrypha, as do the collective names they give them. Many regard them as useful in gaining additional understanding of the Old and New Testaments and the hand of God in history, even if they don’t give them the same status as the 66 books of the Old and New Testaments. They are included here in support of the churches and individuals who read them and use them, as separate from, but frequently used with, the core canon of the 66 books of the Holy Bible.

What are MT, TR, and NU in the footnotes?
  • MT refers to the Byzantine Greek Majority Text New Testament, which is usually in the main text.
  • TR stands for Textus Receptus, which is the Greek Text from which the King James Version New Testament was translated.
  • NU stands for the Nestle-Aland/UBS critical text of the Greek New Testament, which is used as a basis for some other Bible translations.
