†1:1 “The Logos”: Traditionally translated as “the Word,” the Greek term Logos defies a simple English equivalent. For John's readers, this was not a reference to spoken speech or a written text, but to the ultimate Divine Expression and Revelation of God. It carries the dual weight of the Old Testament Wisdom that fashioned the universe (Proverbs 8) and the living, personal Word of Yahweh that took physical action in the prophets—such as stretching out a hand to touch Jeremiah (Jeremiah 1:9). By using Logos, John declares that the cosmic structure of reality and the personal expression of God have taken on human flesh in Jesus.

†1:5 The word translated “overcome” (κατέλαβεν) can also be translated “comprehended.” It refers to getting a grip on an enemy to defeat him.

‡1:14 The phrase “only born” is from the Greek word “μονογενους”, which is sometimes translated “only begotten” or “one and only”.

§1:17 Messiah (Hebrew) and Christ (Greek) both literally mean “Anointed One.” In reality, the word refers to a King—a special king, who was to be born according to God's promise, in king David's lineage to rule forever.

†1:18 The phrase “only born” is from the Greek word “μονογενη”, which is sometimes translated “only begotten” or “one and only”.

‡1:18 NU reads “God”

†1:23 Yahweh: Gk. Kýrios (Κύριος), "Lord." The text follows the Septuagint (LXX Isaiah 40:3), which employs Kýrios as a surrogate for the Hebrew Tetragrammaton (יהוה / YHWH) in accordance with second-temple Jewish traditions of qere/ketiv (where Adonai was read aloud in place of the written divine name). By translating the source text directly from the MT (Masoretic Text: קוֹל קוֹרֵא בַּמִּדְבָּר פַּנּוּ דֶּרֶךְ יְהוָה), "Yahweh" is restored to reflect the original prophetic context. Within the Johannine theological framework, this citation carries profound Christological weight: by identifying Yeshua (Yēšûaʿ) as the coming one whose way is being prepared, the Evangelist explicitly applies the divine name and covenantal identity of Yahweh directly to Yeshua, establishing a high Christology from the prologue onward (cf. Isa 40:3; Mal 3:1).

§1:29 “Behold”, from “ἰδοὺ”, means look at, take notice, observe, see, or gaze at. It is often used as an interjection.

‡1:41 Messiah (Hebrew) and Christ (Greek) both literally mean “Anointed One.” In reality, the word refers to a King—a special king, who was to be born according to God's promise, in king David's lineage to rule forever.

§1:42 “Cephas” (Aramaic) and “Peter” (Greek) both mean “Rock”.
